What does NASA EONET actually track?

This is not a list of disasters today. EONET catalogs open natural events such as wildfires, storms, floods and volcanoes. Up to 100 currently open records are displayed. A date can refer to the most recent recorded position, not the event start.

🔥 Wildfire – an event record, not a damage estimate.🌪️ Cyclone – consult weather agencies for intensity.🌿 Prescribed fire – planned vegetation management, not necessarily an emergency.

The count is not a worldwide total. Open events can be days old. Follow NASA links to primary records.

Values are measurements or catalog counts, not forecasts. EONET lists open events and is not an exhaustive list of events on Earth.

11 / ABOUT THE PROJECT

Why we started

It started with a question: What if we notice individual events but miss the relationships between them?

Curiosity, not ready-made answers

Conversations about nature, science, human intuition and world events led to an idea: an open observatory that compares independently verifiable measurements, public records and observations submitted by people around the world.

O.C.D.26 began as a human–AI concept in November 2026. We are still building it. We do not promise to predict disasters or reveal hidden truths.

You do not have to believe. You do not have to disbelieve. You can observe, question and discover.

Our mission

Observe — record what happened.
Connect — compare independent sources.
Discover — ask better questions, rather than sell sensational answers.

You do not need a scientific degree to contribute an observation or help examine a finding.

WHY WE STARTED

Information belongs to everyone. Conclusions are yours to make.

We create space to distinguish facts from interpretations, explore connections, and preserve independent judgment.

Why O.C.D.26 exists — read more

O.C.D.26 grew from a simple principle: information and interpretation are not the same. We aim to explore without predetermined conclusions, verify sources, and acknowledge uncertainty.

We do not offer one approved version of the truth. We place facts, interpretations, and open questions side by side.

You do not have to agree with us. Curiosity, respectful discussion, and a willingness to reconsider are enough.

We do not seek to prove something is happening. We seek not to miss the moment when something truly begins.
08 / OUR METHOD

Not just what happened. What can we learn?

Three layers of every finding

  1. Observation: What did an instrument measure or a verifiable record actually show?
  2. Correlation: Do two phenomena recur together when compared against a historical baseline?
  3. Hypothesis: What could explain the pattern, and what would disprove it?
Similar timing does not prove a common cause.

Explanation, not sensationalism

A future live report might read: “Geomagnetic activity is above its typical range. NOAA measured the value at XX:XX. There is currently no evidence of a related global power outage.”

This example demonstrates how findings will be explained. It is not a current observation.

Where the information comes from

Select any organization to visit its official website. We explain what it measures and whether its data are actually connected. A source link does not confirm a hypothesis.

Publication standard: source first

Every factual report must identify its source, direct record URL and publication date. Measurements additionally need a timestamp and unit. A homepage link alone is not evidence for a specific claim. AI analysis and unverified claims must be labelled.
